## What you need

Your current CV, useful career notes, and the full job description and person specification. For a general CV, choose a target role, level and sector. The separate plain-text prompt file is also available for easy copying in the original resource set.

## Before you start

Follow this simple process:

1. Open one conversation and provide the documents requested by Step 1.
2. Copy one full prompt paragraph at a time. Replace all bracketed fields.
3. Answer clarification questions and correct inaccurate information.
4. Approve the work examples before writing the CV, then approve the final text before formatting.
5. Use the companion CV template if useful and follow the employer’s actual application instructions.

### AI disclaimer

AI is a drafting assistant and can misunderstand your experience, invent details, miscount words or overstate your contribution. Review and correct every output before using it. Include only work, qualifications, tools and results that accurately reflect your experience and that you can explain at interview. Never adopt suggested figures or achievements simply because they sound convincing. Do not upload confidential employer, client, patient or service-user information without permission, and remove unnecessary personal identifiers. Follow the employer’s rules on AI assistance, anonymisation and applications. You remain responsible for the accuracy and final submission of your CV. Mettelo and this workflow do not guarantee an interview or job offer.

### If you need a new conversation

Save your target requirements, approved work examples and latest CV. Paste or attach them in the new conversation and say which step you are on. Do not assume the AI remembers earlier conversations.

## 1. Understand the role

**Before you start:** Attach the complete JD and person specification, or enter a target role, level, sector and country.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Help me prepare a specific, credible CV for [TARGET ROLE] at [LEVEL] in [SECTOR AND COUNTRY], using the attached job description, person specification and application instructions where available. Do not write my CV yet. Explain the purpose of the role and identify the seven or eight most important organisational problems, delivery needs or responsibilities it addresses, describing what successful delivery would involve and who would benefit. Separate essential criteria, desirable criteria and contextual information, and identify any mandatory qualification, professional registration or experience requirement. Assess the expected technical or professional capability, independence, judgement, complexity, leadership and stakeholder engagement, distinguishing managing people from influencing colleagues or delivering independently. For each priority requirement, explain what specific career evidence would demonstrate it, such as a process improved, problem resolved, service delivered, decision supported, product developed, risk controlled or team capability strengthened. Identify relevant tools, methods and terminology, but do not treat every tool mentioned as mandatory or suggest keywords unsupported by my experience. Present a concise table showing the requirement, its priority, suitable evidence and the questions you will later use to investigate my background. Separate explicit requirements from your interpretation. Record any stated CV length, format, anonymisation and AI-use instructions; flag missing instructions rather than inventing them. If I have no particular vacancy, use my chosen role, level and sector to create a provisional target-role map and say clearly that it is not an employer’s specification. If you research current vacancies or company context, cite the sources and do not confuse company activities with my personal achievements; if research is unavailable, state that limitation. Finish with a short explanation of what my CV must demonstrate, then wait for my CV and career information.
```

**Check before continuing:** Check that the employer’s requirements and any assumptions are clearly separated.

## 2. Find the strongest work examples

**Before you start:** Attach your CV versions and relevant career notes. Identify outdated details and corrections.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Read my attached CV versions and career notes against the target role already discussed, and identify the strongest seven or eight specific projects, problems solved, service improvements or significant pieces of recurring work across my career. Do not write the CV yet. Review each relevant employer and role separately before selecting the strongest examples overall, including useful earlier experience, volunteering, academic work and independent projects while keeping their contexts clear. For each selected example, provide a descriptive title, employer or experience category, role and approximate date, then explain the original problem, who was affected, why it mattered and the known scale or constraints. Describe what I personally investigated, decided, designed, delivered or improved; the genuine approach, framework, process or tools used; what colleagues contributed; and what changed afterwards. Record any stated outcome, its measurement period, baseline and degree of certainty where available, together with the wider organisational or service benefit. Link each example to the relevant target-role needs and identify the CV section or notes that support it. Use short numbered summaries that I can review easily, rather than a complicated scoring system. Look behind vague phrases such as “managed reporting”, “supported service users” or “improved processes” and explain which details are missing. Do not infer my achievements from an employer’s reputation, website or job description, and do not assign a tool to a project merely because it appears elsewhere in my CV. Distinguish responsibilities, completed actions, team outcomes and future plans. Flag contradictory dates, figures or ownership instead of silently resolving them. Select fewer than seven examples if that is all the material supports, and never invent an example or metric to reach the target. Finish with the most useful clarification questions and wait for my review.
```

**Check before continuing:** Check the examples reflect your actual work and include useful earlier experience.

## 3. Review and strengthen the evidence

**Before you start:** Read the numbered examples and answer questions about your actions and results.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Help me review the shortlisted work examples so that the CV is based on an accurate account I can explain at interview. Work through one example at a time and ask no more than five focused questions in each message. Investigate the specific starting problem, its consequence, my responsibility, the actions and decisions I took, why I chose that approach, the tools or professional methods actually used, any constraints or trade-offs, stakeholder involvement and the checks performed before delivery or handover. Ask what changed afterwards and whether the improvement was sustained, using questions tailored to the example rather than repeatedly presenting a generic questionnaire. When a numerical outcome is mentioned, establish what was measured, the starting and final values, the period, the calculation or record supporting it and whether it is exact, approximate or an estimate. Never suggest an impressive percentage for me to adopt or invent a financial benefit from a time saving. Distinguish an outcome I directly achieved from a team result or a wider change I only contributed towards. If reliable numerical evidence does not exist, ask about a meaningful qualitative outcome such as a completed delivery, standardised process, successful handover or documented improvement, and use it only if I confirm it. Check whether verbs such as led, owned, designed or implemented accurately describe my role. After my answers, provide a corrected summary covering the problem, personal contribution, approach, tools and result, with any remaining uncertainty clearly stated, and ask me to approve or amend it. Mark it approved only after my explicit confirmation; unanswered or rejected claims must remain excluded. Once all examples are reviewed, give me the final approved summaries in one place and wait for the next stage. My approval confirms my account and does not amount to independent verification.
```

**Check before continuing:** Approve the corrected examples before moving to CV writing.

## 4. Choose what belongs in the CV

**Before you start:** Use the approved examples and the target job requirements.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Use my approved work examples and confirmed target-role requirements to decide what belongs in the CV. Do not draft the CV yet. Match the most important essential and desirable requirements to the strongest relevant examples, explaining briefly whether the evidence is direct, transferable or insufficient. Prioritise relevance, my actual contribution, meaningful outcomes and the expected level of responsibility rather than simply selecting the largest number or most fashionable tool. Identify the three strongest evidence-based reasons my background fits this role and the specific details that make my experience more informative than a generic duty list, such as a difficult constraint, decision, useful scale, professional approach, sustained improvement or successful adoption. Highlight requirements I cannot currently demonstrate, especially mandatory qualifications or registration, without disguising the gap through keywords. Recommend which examples should receive the most space, which overlap and should be combined or reduced, which useful earlier achievements should remain, and whether projects or volunteering add enough distinct value to justify a separate section. Propose a clear section order and a sensible bullet allocation for each role based on relevance; do not give every job the same number of bullets or require each shortlisted example to become exactly one bullet. Follow the application’s length and anonymisation instructions. If no length is stated, propose a concise working length appropriate to my experience and explain any necessary trade-off. Preserve accurate job titles, dates and career context rather than changing them to imitate the vacancy. Present a simple CV outline, a brief requirement-to-example comparison and any final questions that would materially improve the application. Do not create an ATS score, claim that I am unique or predict interview success. Ask me to approve the outline before drafting.
```

**Check before continuing:** Approve the section order and strongest evidence; address important gaps honestly.

## 5. Write the complete CV

**Before you start:** Confirm the outline, qualifications, dates and contact details. Use the companion Word template if helpful.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Write my complete CV for the target role using only the approved outline, reviewed work examples and confirmed career facts in this conversation, following the actual application’s length, format and anonymisation instructions. Use clear sections for contact details, professional profile, relevant skills, employment, useful projects or volunteering, and education or certifications, removing sections that add little. Write a profile of approximately 40 to 60 words explaining my relevant background, the needs I can address and two or three supported strengths, avoiding clichés and unsupported seniority; do not double-count overlapping jobs when stating experience. Group relevant skills naturally and do not turn basic exposure or training into professional expertise. For employment and project achievements, write each bullet in 15 to 25 words, connecting a specific problem or delivery need with my personal action and a meaningful result, including the genuine approach, framework or tools where they explain the achievement. Use the full approved evidence to select the strongest details rather than cramming every field into every sentence. Prefer precise verbs, relevant scope and a verified outcome, with a truthful qualitative result where no defensible metric exists. Preserve approximate figures, team attribution and the difference between direct impact and contribution; never upgrade supported to led, helped develop to architected, or used to implemented unless my account supports it. Avoid vague duties, disconnected tool lists, repeated results, inflated adjectives and unnecessary jargon. Put the strongest relevant bullets first within each role, use accurate titles and dates, and label academic, independent and voluntary work honestly. Keep qualifications, institutions and completion status accurate. Count achievement-bullet words using whitespace for this review, correcting bullets outside the range. Provide the clean CV first, then a short separate list of any unresolved questions or claims needing my confirmation; do not include evidence labels, word counts, review notes or invented personal details inside the CV. Wait for my review.
```

**Check before continuing:** Read every claim and check that the wording reflects your real contribution.

## 6. Check and improve the draft

**Before you start:** Use the latest CV draft, approved examples and application instructions.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Review my draft CV critically against the actual job requirements, approved work examples and application instructions, then improve it using only supported information. Start with accuracy: check personal versus team responsibility, job titles, dates, qualifications, tools, exact versus estimated metrics, calculations and any claim that one action caused a wider result. Identify anything introduced during drafting that I did not confirm, and remove it or ask me a focused question. Assess whether the CV clearly demonstrates the essential requirements and expected level of responsibility, whether useful earlier evidence is visible, and whether the strongest examples receive appropriate space. Flag mandatory requirements I cannot demonstrate rather than disguising them. Identify generic bullets that could describe almost anyone and strengthen them with approved details about the problem, approach, tools, scope and outcome; if those details are missing, ask rather than inventing them. Check every achievement bullet contains 15 to 25 whitespace-separated words, retains its meaning and uses a precise verb consistent with my actual contribution. Remove repetition, unsupported adjectives, unnecessary jargon, duplicated achievements and keywords that overstate my skills. Ensure the profile is specific and introduces no new claims, the skills reflect real use or accurately labelled learning, and career progression is shown through supported responsibility. Check the actual length, anonymisation and file instructions, spelling, tense, contact details, links, qualification status and unresolved placeholders. Give me a short list of the most important issues and corrections, prioritising factual concerns over style, followed by a corrected complete CV. Keep review comments outside the CV. Do not invent an ATS score, claim that a layout guarantees screening success or predict shortlisting. Ask me to approve the final wording before export, and do not treat an earlier AI draft as independent evidence that a claim is true.
```

**Check before continuing:** Resolve factual concerns and approve the final text before formatting.

## 7. Format and prepare to apply

**Before you start:** Use the final approved text and the employer’s required file format.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Prepare my approved CV for submission using the companion Word template or a clean single-column layout, while preserving the approved facts and following the employer’s actual instructions. Use readable type, standard section headings, consistent dates, ordinary bullets and clear spacing, keeping essential contact and employment information in the main document body. Remove instructions, placeholders, review comments, evidence labels and word counts, and do not carry the Mettelo guide’s logo or preparation credit into my application CV. Keep the agreed 15 to 25 word achievement bullets. If the document exceeds its permitted length, recommend shortening less relevant content before making the text difficult to read, and do not silently delete important evidence or change facts to fit. If file creation is available, produce an editable Word CV and a PDF where requested or permitted, render and inspect every page for clipping, awkward breaks and readable headings, confirm that PDF text is selectable and copies in a sensible order, and check contact and portfolio links. Do not claim files have been created or visually checked unless those actions were actually completed; if the tools are unavailable, provide the clean final text and practical formatting instructions. Use a filename such as [Name]_[Target_Role]_CV_[YYYY-MM-DD], or the employer’s naming instructions for a blind application, and confirm the final file follows its requested format. Keep the approved work summaries and submitted CV separately for future tailoring and interview preparation. Finish with a short submission check covering the correct vacancy, final version, contact details, remaining placeholders, application instructions and correct attachment. Remind me to open and review the final document myself. Do not submit the application on my behalf or claim that following this workflow guarantees an interview.
```

**Check before continuing:** Open the final file and check every page before submitting it yourself.

## Final checklist

- [ ] The CV addresses the correct role and its important requirements.
- [ ] Every claim reflects your actual experience and approved work examples.
- [ ] Your contribution, team results and any estimates are described accurately.
- [ ] Achievement bullets contain 15 to 25 words and communicate specific actions and outcomes.
- [ ] Dates, qualifications, job titles, contact details and links are accurate.
- [ ] The file follows the employer’s format, length, anonymisation and AI-use instructions.
- [ ] All placeholders, review notes and guide branding have been removed.
- [ ] You have opened the final file, checked every page and selected the correct attachment.

## Using the CV template

`Mettelo_CV_Template.docx` is an editable starting point. Replace all placeholders and remove unused sections. Adjust its length to the vacancy and your relevant experience; its two-page structure is not a universal requirement.

## For your next application

Reuse the approved work examples. Start at Step 1 for the new vacancy, then use Steps 4 to 7. Return to Steps 2 and 3 when adding or correcting experience.
