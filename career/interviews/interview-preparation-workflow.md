## How to use this guide

1. Gather the full JD, person specification, interview invitation, submitted CV and application. Add any assessment instructions.
2. Use one conversation. Copy the complete paragraph beneath “Copy this prompt” and replace bracketed details.
3. Review each response, correct mistakes and answer clarification questions before continuing.
4. Confirm your work examples before developing answers, then practise aloud using short cues.
5. If starting a new conversation, provide the interview requirements, confirmed examples and latest preparation notes again.

### AI disclaimer

AI is a preparation assistant and can misunderstand your experience, invent facts, suggest unsuitable answers or give unreliable feedback. Review every output and use only genuine examples you can explain and defend. Suggested questions are practice questions, not confirmed interview questions; feedback is not an employer’s score or a prediction of success. Never invent achievements, responsibilities, tools, figures or motivation. Do not upload confidential employer, client, patient or service-user information without permission, and remove unnecessary personal identifiers. Follow the employer’s rules on AI, assessments and permitted materials. Use this guide to prepare; do not use undisclosed live AI assistance during an interview or restricted assessment. You remain responsible for your answers and conduct. Mettelo and this guide do not guarantee an interview pass or job offer.

### Keep your preparation simple

You need a small set of strong examples, a clear introduction and practice explaining decisions. Keep the detailed work summaries separately and use the final sheet for brief reminders. The companion plain-text file contains the same prompts for easy copying.

## Choose the right practice approach

Use only the interview types that match your invitation. The employer’s instructions take priority. Step 5 adapts the questions and answer structure to the relevant type.

### Experience and competency questions

Keep the situation and task brief. Explain your personal actions and decisions, the result and what you learned. For a Civil Service behaviour or another named framework, provide the actual definition and level so practice can address it accurately.

### Technical questions and practical tasks

Clarify the problem and assumptions, explain your approach, work through the solution, test it and discuss limitations or trade-offs. Prepare to explain why you chose a method, not only which tool you used.

### Presentations and take home assessments

Work from the actual brief and assistance rules. Prepare a clear recommendation, supporting reasoning, validation and limitations. Rehearse the stated timing, transitions and likely challenges, including what you would change with more time.

### Scenarios and case studies

Ask useful clarifications, identify priorities and constraints, compare options and explain a practical recommendation, risks and next steps. Make hypothetical assumptions explicit rather than presenting the scenario as something you personally delivered.

### Strengths and motivation

Explain your real interests, preferences and reasons for applying. Use a brief example when useful. Do not force a long STAR answer into a direct motivation question or invent enthusiasm for a company priority you do not understand.

## 1. Understand the interview

**Before you start:** Attach the JD, person specification, interview invitation and any assessment instructions.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Help me prepare for an interview for [ROLE] at [LEVEL] with [ORGANISATION], using the attached job description, person specification, interview invitation and assessment instructions. Do not invent interview details or draft answers yet. Identify the interview format, duration, stages, assessed criteria, presentation or technical requirements, stated assessment framework and any restrictions on materials or AI assistance. Separate confirmed information from assumptions and list important details that remain unknown. Explain the purpose of the role and the main problems or delivery needs the employer wants this person to address, then prioritise the essential requirements and expected level of judgement, independence, complexity, technical capability, stakeholder engagement and leadership. Distinguish a people-management requirement from influencing others or delivering independently. Explain the kinds of evidence that could demonstrate each priority area without assuming a specific scoring method. Create a concise preparation plan based on my interview date [DATE], time available [HOURS OR DAYS] and areas of concern [DETAILS], giving practical priorities rather than a long generic syllabus. If company research would help and browsing is available, use official sources to identify relevant services, products, priorities and values, cite the links and distinguish verified facts from inference; if research is unavailable, say so and ask me to provide the relevant information. Do not treat an organisation’s public achievements as my personal experience. Identify what I may need to confirm with the organiser, including presentation length, equipment or adjustment arrangements where relevant, but do not contact anyone on my behalf. Finish with a short summary of what the interview is likely to test, clearly label that interpretation, and ask me for my submitted application, CV and career examples.
```

**Check before continuing:** Confirm the format, assessed areas, timing and anything you still need to clarify.

## 2. Select relevant work examples

**Before you start:** Provide the CV and application you submitted, plus useful career or project notes.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Review my submitted CV, application and career notes against the interview requirements already identified, and select the strongest six to eight genuine examples I could discuss. Do not invent achievements or write polished answers yet. Look across relevant current and earlier employment, projects, volunteering and academic work, keeping each context accurate. For each selected example, give a short descriptive title, role and approximate date, the specific problem, its consequence and known scale, my stated responsibility, the actions or decisions I took, the genuine approach or tools used and the result or lesson. Distinguish what I personally did from the wider team’s work and separate completed results from planned work. Explain which assessment areas and possible question types each example could support, such as delivery, collaboration, difficult decisions, improvement, technical problem-solving or learning from setbacks, choosing categories appropriate to the actual role rather than forcing every example into a fixed framework. Include a difficult or unsuccessful piece of work where it offers credible evidence of judgement, recovery and learning; do not manufacture a failure or turn every story into an unrealistic success. Flag missing details, contradictory figures or unsupported ownership and identify important requirements for which I have little evidence. Do not assign a tool or method to an example merely because it appears elsewhere in my CV or the employer’s job description. Use simple numbered summaries rather than an elaborate scorecard. Select fewer examples if that is all my materials support, preserve useful alternatives and avoid repeatedly splitting the same project into nearly identical stories. Finish with focused questions that would improve the examples and wait for my corrections.
```

**Check before continuing:** Check the examples are genuine and relevant; correct dates, scope and ownership.

## 3. Strengthen and confirm your examples

**Before you start:** Review the shortlist and answer questions about what you did and why.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Help me strengthen the shortlisted interview examples through an honest review of the facts. Work through one example at a time and ask no more than five focused questions per message. Investigate what was happening before I became involved, why it mattered, my actual responsibility, the steps I took, decisions I made, alternatives considered, constraints or trade-offs, relevant methods and tools, stakeholders involved, and checks used to ensure quality. Ask what went wrong or remained limited, how I responded, how I handed over or sustained the work, and what I would do differently now. For numerical claims, establish the baseline, final figure, measurement period, source or calculation, and whether the result is exact, approximate or estimated; never suggest an impressive figure for me to adopt. Distinguish direct impact from a wider outcome I only contributed towards, and use a confirmed qualitative result where no reliable metric exists. Probe whether words such as led, owned, designed or implemented match my actual contribution. For a technical example, investigate testing, validation, assumptions, failure handling and maintenance where relevant; for service or people-focused work, investigate the professional judgement, escalation, communication and practical outcome without inferring unmeasured safety or clinical benefits. After my answers, produce a concise corrected summary covering the problem, personal action, reasoning, result, limitations and learning. Ask me to approve or amend it, and mark it confirmed only after my explicit response. Keep unanswered claims out of later answers. Once the examples are reviewed, collect the confirmed summaries in one place and identify any material gaps still needing preparation. My confirmation is a review of my account, not independent verification.
```

**Check before continuing:** Approve the corrected examples before using them to build answers.

## 4. Prepare Tell me about yourself

**Before you start:** Use your confirmed experience and explain your actual career goal and interest in the organisation.

> **Introduction formula:** Introduction → Background → Problem solved → Skills developed → Future goal and why this company

### Copy this prompt

```text
Using the job description, person specification, my submitted CV and confirmed career examples, help me prepare a natural answer to “Tell me about yourself” using this exact sequence: introduction, relevant background, a specific problem I solved, the skills I developed through solving it, and my future goal connected to why I want to join this company. Introduce my professional identity, accurate relevant experience and current focus, then briefly explain the background that prepared me for this opportunity without repeating my entire employment history. Select one strong relevant problem and explain the challenge, what I personally did, the approach or tools used where helpful and the meaningful outcome. Connect that experience to the technical, professional or interpersonal skills I developed or strengthened and show how they prepare me for this role, rather than adding a disconnected list of skills. Finish with what I genuinely want to contribute and develop next, why the advertised responsibilities fit that direction and what specifically attracts me to the organisation. Ask me about missing motivation or career-goal information instead of inventing it. Use supplied company information or verified official sources for company-specific claims and acknowledge any research limitation. Preserve the distinction between my work and team outcomes and avoid clichés, exaggerated seniority or unnecessary personal information. Draft one connected paragraph suitable as a starting point for approximately 60 to 90 seconds of speaking, then provide five short speaking cues corresponding to the formula and a shorter version for a brief introduction. Let me practise in my own words before giving feedback on relevance, clarity, authenticity and length; if I provide text only, do not claim to have measured my speaking pace or delivery. Keep a fuller explanation of motivation available for a separate “Why this role or company?” question.
```

**Check before continuing:** Practise aloud and check it sounds like you, connects to the role and fits the time.

## 5. Prepare questions and clear answers

**Before you start:** Choose the interview type that applies and use the confirmed examples.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Prepare a focused set of practice questions for my interview using the confirmed assessment areas, role level and supplied instructions, labelling them as plausible practice questions rather than actual or leaked questions. Group a manageable selection by the requirements that matter most, explain briefly what each question is testing and identify suitable confirmed examples without forcing one example into every topic. Include relevant follow-ups that probe what I personally did, why I chose the approach, alternatives, quality checks, difficulties, results and learning. Include motivation, “Why this organisation?”, a relevant setback or development area and questions I can ask the panel where appropriate. Adapt the answer structure to the question: use brief context, task, detailed personal actions, result and reflection for experience questions; clarify the problem, assumptions, approach, testing and trade-offs for technical questions; explore options, risks, recommendation and implementation for hypothetical scenarios; and use a direct personal answer with concise evidence for strengths or motivation. Do not force every response into STAR or assume a framework not supplied by the employer. Show the questions first and ask me which to practise. For each selected question, invite my own first attempt, then help me build a natural answer using only confirmed information and an appropriate working length or the stated time limit. Distinguish an improved answer based on my experience from a hypothetical illustration. Provide short speaking notes and likely follow-ups, avoiding scripts that I must memorise word for word. If a technical task or presentation is part of the interview, prepare me to explain the brief, decisions, validation, limitations and recommendations, and respect all stated rules about assistance. Flag evidence gaps and ask for missing facts rather than manufacturing stronger answers.
```

**Check before continuing:** Check the answer addresses the actual question and shows your reasoning.

## 6. Run a realistic mock interview

**Before you start:** Choose a realistic practice duration and state whether you will type or speak your answers.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Run a realistic mock interview for the target role using the confirmed format, requirements and my reviewed experience. Practice duration is [DURATION]; I will [TYPE OR SPEAK] my answers, and I want [NUMBER] main questions if the format permits. Ask one main question at a time and wait for my response. Do not reveal a model answer, ideal structure or coaching before I answer. Ask relevant follow-up questions, one at a time, to test my personal contribution, reasoning, alternatives, risks, quality checks, outcome and learning, adapting to what I actually say rather than using the same probes for every example. Mix question types only where they match the stated interview and avoid introducing assessment areas as if the employer had confirmed them. If I cannot answer, record the gap and move on appropriately without supplying invented personal evidence. For a technical or case question, let me clarify the brief, explain assumptions and work through my approach before challenging it. Use the supplied assessment framework for evaluation if one exists; otherwise explain that feedback is qualitative practice feedback and do not invent an official score, pass mark or chance of success. Keep coaching until the end of the mock unless I explicitly ask to switch to coaching mode. After the final question, review each answer with a specific strength, a quoted or accurately paraphrased weakness and a practical improvement, distinguishing missing evidence from unclear communication. Identify the three highest-priority improvements and the questions I should retry. If you receive typed answers only, assess their content and organisation but do not claim to have observed tone, eye contact, body language or actual speaking duration. Ask me to retry before showing any improved answer, and base later improvements only on my confirmed facts.
```

**Check before continuing:** Complete the mock before reading improved answers or retrying weak questions.

## 7. Improve weak answers and practise again

**Before you start:** Use the mock feedback and select one answer to improve at a time.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Use the mock interview feedback to coach me on the highest-priority weaknesses, working on one answer at a time. Identify whether the problem is missing evidence, a failure to answer the question, unclear personal ownership, weak reasoning, excessive background, unsupported impact or poor organisation. Quote or accurately paraphrase the part of my answer that needs work and explain what an interviewer still needs to understand, without claiming to know their actual judgement. Ask focused questions to recover genuine detail before rewriting and never fill a gap by inventing a responsibility, decision, tool, result or motivation. Help me give proportionate context, explain what I personally did and why, demonstrate the result or learning and connect it to the question, using the relevant technical, experience, scenario or motivation structure. Where I do not know a technical answer, help me practise stating the boundary of my knowledge, asking a useful clarification and explaining how I would investigate, rather than bluffing. Where an example was unsuccessful, preserve the failure and focus on judgement, recovery and genuine learning rather than rewriting it into a success. Invite a second attempt before providing a revised example answer, then compare the two for specificity, relevance, credibility and clarity. Offer concise speaking cues that I can personalise instead of a polished script to memorise. Ask fresh follow-ups so I practise adapting rather than repeating the same words. Measure duration only if actual timed practice is available; otherwise ask me to time myself and report the result. Finish each coaching cycle with one clear improvement to retain and one remaining action, and stop expanding an answer once it addresses the question effectively.
```

**Check before continuing:** Make sure the new answer is more specific, accurate and natural rather than merely longer.

## 8. Complete your final preparation

**Before you start:** Check the invitation, practical arrangements, outstanding gaps and questions for the panel.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Help me complete final preparation for the confirmed interview without adding unsupported information or creating an unnecessarily long revision pack. Produce a concise preparation sheet containing my introduction cues in the agreed sequence, the priority assessment areas, the confirmed work examples and the key decisions, results and lessons I need to remember, a few verified organisation facts relevant to the role, and the remaining gaps I should address. Suggest three or four thoughtful questions for the panel about priorities, success in the role, current challenges, collaboration or development, tailoring them to the JD and avoiding questions already answered in the invitation; do not invent company circumstances to make a question sound specific. Check the practical arrangements stated in my invitation, including date, time zone, location or meeting link, presentation and technical requirements, documents requested, permitted notes or tools, and any adjustments I have arranged. Do not assume an online or in-person format where it is unconfirmed. For online practice, remind me to check the meeting link, microphone, camera and screen sharing where relevant; for an in-person interview, identify the journey and arrival arrangements I need to confirm without inventing travel details. Prioritise a short final rehearsal of my introduction, one strong example, one challenging follow-up and any presentation opening or conclusion, rather than trying to memorise every answer. Remind me to follow the employer’s rules on AI and assessment assistance and not use undisclosed live AI support. Keep facts, example notes and practice feedback distinct, and do not predict the result. Finish with a short personal readiness checklist. After the interview, help me record questions, what went well and development points from my recollection, while respecting any confidentiality restrictions.
```

**Check before continuing:** Keep a short preparation sheet and review the employer’s rules before the interview.

## Final preparation sheet

Keep this short. Fill in cues rather than complete scripts. Use notes during the interview only where the employer permits them.

### Introduction cues

```text
Introduction: [identity and focus]
Background: [relevant experience]
Problem solved: [challenge, personal action and result]
Skills developed: [capabilities strengthened]
Future goal and why this company: [contribution, direction and specific connection]
```

### Examples to remember

For each priority requirement, note the example title, your key decision, actual result and learning. Keep one alternative for an example that has already been discussed.

### Questions for the panel

Choose three or four useful questions based on what remains unclear about the role. Listen during the interview and adapt them if the panel has already answered them.

### Ready to attend

- [ ] Date, time zone, meeting link or location and attendance instructions checked.
- [ ] Presentation, technical setup, requested documents and any adjustments confirmed.
- [ ] Introduction and relevant examples practised aloud, including difficult follow-ups.
- [ ] Important company claims verified and personal motivations genuine.
- [ ] Notes, tools and AI assistance comply with the employer’s instructions.
- [ ] Answers contain only experience and outcomes you can explain truthfully.
