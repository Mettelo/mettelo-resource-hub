# LinkedIn Profile Editing Guide

**Make your work understandable and your claims believable**

Mettelo | Revised edition 2 | 24 October 2026  
Prepared by Johnson Taiwo

A useful profile lets someone understand what you do, what you have changed and whether your experience fits their needs. This guide shows the actual edits through one worked example, with alternatives for different career situations.

## The worked example

Ruth is a **fictional Operations Analyst**. Three regional teams prepared monthly reports in inconsistent spreadsheets. She wrote SQL validation checks and built Power BI dashboards; colleagues agreed definitions and tested outputs. Preparation fell from two working days to four hours across three monthly reporting cycles. She wants reporting and analysis roles.

> All names, figures and sample achievements in this guide are illustrations. Use the editing method; do not copy the fictional experience into your profile. The full AI disclaimer is at the end.

---

## Give the headline a clear job

### The weak version

> Results-driven professional | Data | AI | Python | SQL | Power BI | Open to opportunities

This introduces AI and Python without evidence from Ruth’s example. It lists interests but leaves the reader to guess what she actually delivers.

### Three defensible directions for Ruth

**Identify the work**

> Operations Analyst | SQL and Power BI | Monthly performance reporting

Useful when she wants to make her current capability easy to recognise.

**Name the contribution**

> Operations analytics | Making monthly reporting consistent with SQL and Power BI

Useful when the reporting problem is central to the roles she wants.

**Emphasise the setting**

> Operations Analyst | Reporting across regional teams | SQL validation and Power BI

Useful when cross-team reporting and reliable definitions are relevant.

### Change the substance for a different role

- **Analytics engineer:** emphasise tested data models or reliable reporting datasets if you actually build them.
- **Business analyst:** emphasise requirements, process decisions or service change you have delivered.
- **Care professional:** emphasise your actual care setting and responsibilities, without implying clinical registration you do not hold.

Keep one clear direction. Use the live headline field limit. A broader professional description in the headline does not justify changing an official job title in Experience.

## Let the About section explain one contribution

### A fictional About section for Ruth

> I work on the reporting problems that make it difficult for operations teams to compare performance consistently.
>
> In my Operations Analyst role, three regional teams were preparing monthly reports in separate spreadsheets. I wrote SQL validation checks and built Power BI dashboards, working with colleagues to agree definitions and test the outputs. Across three monthly reporting cycles, preparation fell from two working days to four hours.
>
> That work strengthened my ability to connect data checks, clear definitions and practical reporting. It also taught me that a dashboard is only useful when the people using it understand and trust the information behind it.
>
> I am interested in reporting and analysis roles where I can improve the reliability and usefulness of operational information. I welcome conversations about reporting quality, SQL and Power BI.

### Why these details earn their place

- The opening identifies the work rather than claiming a personality trait.
- The example names her action, the team’s involvement and the measured period.
- The closing gives a relevant direction and a specific reason to connect.

### What still needs Ruth’s confirmation

Even in this illustration, the learning and motivation paragraphs would need Ruth’s approval. A writing tool can propose a reflection, but cannot know what someone actually learned or wants.

For your own version, choose one example you can discuss in detail. If there is no reliable number, explain an approved qualitative result. Do not add a percentage merely to make the paragraph look stronger.

## Turn duties into an account of delivery

### Keep the official role

**Operations Analyst | [Actual employer] | [Accurate dates]**

Scope: Monthly operational reporting for three regional teams.

### Replace a vague duty

**Revised:** “Wrote SQL validation checks and built Power BI dashboards, reducing monthly report preparation from two working days to four hours.”

**A complementary contribution:** “Worked with regional colleagues to agree reporting definitions and test dashboard outputs before adoption across three teams.”

### Keep the boundary of the claim

The evidence supports reporting and validation work. It does not establish that Ruth designed an enterprise data platform, managed the whole programme or delivered a financial saving. Changing the verb cannot supply missing experience.

### Write for the work you actually do

- **For analysis,** explain the decision or reporting need, the method and the useful finding or delivery.
- **For engineering,** explain the data flow or model, reliability checks, failure handling and operational result where supported.
- **For service delivery,** explain the specific need, your judgement or action, collaboration and the observed service outcome.

Choose a few distinct contributions for each relevant role. LinkedIn entries need enough context to stand alone; they do not need a fixed number of bullets or a copied two-page CV.

## Match the professional claim to the career stage

### An experienced practitioner

Lead with the work you already deliver and the problems you understand. For a data analyst, show what the analysis changed or enabled; a long software list cannot establish that contribution.

### Someone moving from analyst to engineer

Keep the employment title accurate. Separate production SQL or pipeline work performed at work from engineering skills practised in personal projects. Describe the project architecture, tests and limitations without claiming production scale you have not handled.

### A newcomer using projects to build experience

Use a factual project description such as “Personal project using public transport data”. Explain the question, method, outputs and checks. Do not invent a client, commercial deployment or business impact. Your prior work may still demonstrate relevant judgement and collaboration.

### A senior specialist or manager

Make responsibility visible: what you decided, the constraints you balanced, who you influenced and how quality or delivery was maintained. A senior-sounding headline should be supported by that scope in Experience.

### Choose skills that have somewhere to point

For Ruth, SQL, Power BI, data validation and reporting are supported by the example. Python, machine learning and line management are not established by it. Other verified experience could support them; a desired role alone cannot.

For each important skill, ask: where did I use it, what did I do with it, and how would I explain that use? Check qualification names and completion status separately. An endorsement or a course title does not establish expert proficiency.

## Give Featured items something worth opening

Choose work that helps a reader inspect a claim in your profile. LinkedIn’s Featured section can include work samples such as documents, links, posts, articles and media; see official Help C and D below.

### A reporting case study

**Suggested title:** “Making monthly reporting consistent across regional teams”. The first page should explain the problem, your role, the method, checks, result and limitations. Include screenshots only where sharing is permitted.

**Caption direction:** “An approved case study explaining my SQL validation and Power BI reporting work, including colleague review and the measured preparation-time change.” Use this wording only if such a permitted case study actually exists.

### A demonstration built with public data

**Suggested title:** “SQL reporting validation demonstration using public data”. Include a README explaining the source, assumptions, how to run it, tests and known limitations. Label it as a demonstration rather than a replica of a confidential production system.

### A genuine presentation or article

Explain the subject, intended audience and your contribution. A presentation link should open the actual material you have permission to share, not a claim of speaking experience with no supporting item.

### Inspect the destination

- Open the link as a viewer and check that the intended content is accessible.
- Confirm attribution, data permissions and whether the work belongs to an employer or team.
- Remove credentials, private records and identifying information before any permitted publication.

If no suitable material exists, prepare one honest work sample first. A missing Featured item is preferable to fabricated evidence. Do not imply that a private employer project must be made public to support your career.

## Choose what to show and apply the edits

### Visual presentation

Use a photo that accurately represents you. A banner can state your actual professional focus or display work you have permission to show. Avoid employer logos, credentials or partnership claims that suggest an affiliation you do not have. Check the result on your phone as well as a larger screen.

### Make the changes deliberately

LinkedIn’s desktop Help directs users through Me, View Profile and the relevant section’s edit control. Paste the approved text into the right fields and save. In the app, open your profile through your photo and use the relevant edit icon. Some mobile-browser sections have editing limitations. [A]

For missing sections and Featured items, use Add profile section and the available prompts; use the current Featured controls to manage work samples. [B C]

### Visibility is a choice

Review public profile settings, contact information, profile-update sharing and job preferences separately. Choose the contact route you want available; do not publish a home address or private number simply to make the profile look complete.

Public profile controls affect what is eligible to appear outside LinkedIn’s signed-in view. A preview does not guarantee what every viewer will see. Recruiter-only Open to Work also does not guarantee complete privacy from your employer. [E F]

### Check the saved version

Read the headline and opening About lines together. Open each work link. Confirm that titles, dates and qualifications agree with your actual record. Ask one trusted reader what work they think you do; if their answer differs from your intention, revise the wording rather than adding more buzzwords.

## Writing briefs for the main profile text

Paste your profile text, CV and confirmed facts into one conversation. Choose the brief for the section you are editing. Each brief is one complete paragraph for copying.

### Diagnose the profile

```text
Review the LinkedIn headline, About section, experience and work links I paste below against my target audience [AUDIENCE] and target opportunities [ROLES OR WORK]. Do not rewrite the entire profile immediately. Tell me what a reader can actually establish about the work I do, the problems I solve, the level of responsibility I have and the evidence supporting my claims. Identify the five most important weaknesses in priority order and quote the exact wording that causes each problem. Separate a missing fact from a writing problem and ask no more than five questions to resolve important gaps. Compare my profile with my supplied CV and confirmed career examples, preserving accurate job titles, dates, qualifications and team attribution. Do not infer my achievements from an employer’s reputation or claim to have opened sections you cannot access. Recommend the smallest set of edits that would make my professional direction clearer, explain what each edit should accomplish and wait for me to confirm the facts before drafting.
```

### Choose the headline

```text
Using only my confirmed work and target audience, write three distinct headlines: one identifying my actual professional work, one emphasising a specific problem I address and one combining a credible specialism with the setting in which I use it. For every option, identify the career fact supporting its strongest claim and explain what opportunity it makes relevant. Replace vague promises such as unlocking insights with a concrete contribution where supported, but do not claim commercial services, seniority, AI expertise or a new job title that I have not earned. If I am transitioning, distinguish established experience from skills developed through projects. Keep the wording concise, use a small number of relevant terms naturally and give the character count separately so I can check the live field limit. Do not promise recruiter visibility or an algorithm advantage. Ask me which direction is most accurate and return the approved headline alone after revision.
```

### Write the About section

```text
Write my LinkedIn About section from the confirmed facts below, opening with the specific work or problem I am equipped to address rather than a generic claim about passion. Use one relevant example to explain what was happening, what I personally changed, the approach or tools that mattered and the result I can substantiate. Connect that example to the type of contribution I want to make next without repeating my entire career history or inventing my motivation. Use first-person conversational British English and a few short paragraphs, keeping it concise enough to read comfortably. Preserve approximate measurements, team attribution, employment context and any confidentiality limits. Ask about missing facts instead of adding stronger claims, and do not describe training or a demonstration as professional delivery. Finish with a specific professional interest or invitation that I have actually confirmed, rather than a service promise I do not provide. Give me the draft, flag any sentence needing confirmation and then produce clean final text after my corrections.
```

## Writing briefs for experience and work samples

Use the same confirmed facts. These briefs request targeted edits instead of restarting the whole profile exercise.

### Repair the experience section

```text
Rewrite the relevant LinkedIn Experience entries using my confirmed career facts. Keep the official job title, employer, dates and employment context accurate. Begin each entry with a brief description of the work I was responsible for, then select a few distinct contributions that explain what I personally changed, why the method or decision mattered and the outcome. Give more space to relevant work rather than applying a fixed bullet count or copying my whole CV. Remove repeated duties and vague adjectives, but do not manufacture a result to complete a sentence. Preserve the difference between my action and the team’s outcome, and between measured, approximate and observed results. Recommend skills only where you can point to actual use; distinguish learning, academic work, voluntary work and personal projects from paid experience. If a tool, metric or responsibility is missing, ask me rather than borrowing it from the JD. Return each revised entry separately for copying, followed by a short list of unresolved claims.
```

### Package a work sample

```text
Use the work sample or link I provide to draft a Featured item that a reader can understand without already knowing the project. Explain the original problem, what the item contains, my personal contribution, the key method or decision, a supported result and the main limitation or context. Write a plain-language title and a short description, then identify whether the item is a demonstration, academic exercise, voluntary project, team delivery or production work. Do not invent deployment, clients, users, results or sole ownership. Check the material you can actually access, identify anything still unreviewed and ask me to confirm sharing permission and attribution. Flag confidential information, personal data, credentials or restricted work before publication. If the item is only screenshots, suggest the minimum explanation or accessible supporting material needed to make it useful. Do not pretend a missing repository, case study or demonstration already exists. Finish with the draft caption and only the specific actions required to make this item ready for public viewing.
```

### Review before publishing

```text
Review my final LinkedIn text and selected work links as a reader interested in [TARGET OPPORTUNITIES]. Check whether the headline’s central claim is supported by the About section, experience and work samples, and identify any factual contradiction or unsupported proficiency. Quote remaining generic sentences and replace them only with confirmed detail. Check titles, dates, qualifications, numbers, attribution and contact choices against my supplied facts. Distinguish a visible public proof link from a private example that supports my experience but cannot be published. Give me the final text section by section and a short list of decisions still requiring my input, rather than another full strategy document. When discussing profile settings, use current official LinkedIn Help or acknowledge that the controls have not been verified; public visibility, contact sharing, profile-update notifications and Open to Work are separate choices. Do not promise complete discretion or search ranking. Ask me to inspect the saved profile and links after I make the changes, and do not claim to have published or changed anything unless that actually happened.
```

## Final review and responsible use

### Before publishing

- [ ] The headline’s strongest claim is supported elsewhere in the profile.
- [ ] The About section explains actual work and a genuine professional direction.
- [ ] Titles, dates, figures and qualifications are accurate and consistent.
- [ ] Work samples have truthful labels, clear attribution and sharing permission.
- [ ] Links open correctly and personal visibility choices are intentional.

### AI disclaimer

AI can invent facts, overstate responsibility or produce wording that does not sound like you. Check every draft against your actual experience and only publish claims you can explain. Never adopt fictional examples, suggested figures or invented clients as your own. Do not upload or publish restricted employer, client or personal information without permission. You remain responsible for accuracy, sharing permissions and publication. Mettelo is not affiliated with LinkedIn. This guide does not guarantee views, recruiter contact, interviews or employment.
