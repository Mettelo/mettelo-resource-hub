## Read this first — turn a business idea into credible evidence

**What this visa actually assesses:** The Innovator Founder pathway is for founders creating a genuinely **innovative, viable and scalable** UK venture who play a real day-to-day role in the business. You need an endorsement from a **currently authorised endorsing body** and separate Home Office eligibility, including personal requirements. A company registration, PowerPoint deck, enthusiastic community or AI product label alone does not establish endorsement eligibility. [Immigration Rules Appendix Innovator Founder](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-innovator-founder).

### A practical eight-week founder exercise (not a visa requirement)

| Week | Specific activity | How to do it | What you can actually show |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Define a real UK market problem | Interview 5–10 potential UK customers about recent failed attempts to solve it; record consent and questions | Need statements and interview log, not invented demand |
| 2 | Compare alternatives | Examine three actual competing products: target users, price, functionality, weakness and business model | Evidence-based comparison; clear gap |
| 3 | Test innovation | Identify which **core** business capability is original, difficult to replicate and demonstrably developed by founders | Technical/product concept, IP/know-how plan and genuine founder ownership |
| 4 | Prototype one feature | Build a minimally useful version, with data/licence/security safeguards | Real demonstration and test evidence, not just slides |
| 5 | Recruit pilot testers | Invite genuine prospective users with permission; define what successful use means | Pilot scope, feedback and observed usage |
| 6 | Evaluate costs and founder capability | Estimate build/operating costs using quotes, supplier rates, staffing needs, skills and actual available funds | Viable cost plan with assumptions and uncertainty |
| 7 | Develop UK growth strategy | Build 12–24-month milestones for customers, hires, cash and expansion, with assumptions not guaranteed outcomes | Defensible scalable business plan |
| 8 | Assess endorsement readiness | Check the official authorised endorsers list, their specific criteria, processes and charges; identify gaps before submitting | Documented review and target endorsing body, **not** an endorsement |

### A stronger way to record market interviews

Ask about the interviewee's **recent actual behaviour**: when the problem occurred, current workaround, cost/time consequence, approval process and budget. Avoid leading questions such as “Would you use my amazing AI platform?” Record only consenting, anonymised insights.

**Fictional example:** A UK SME manually reconciles orders from two channels. The applicant's possible innovation might be an original reconciliation mechanism that deals with ambiguous identifiers and produces auditable corrections. They would still need to prove why the approach differs meaningfully from existing commercial products, that their team can build it, that customers genuinely need it and that UK growth assumptions are realistic. A generic integration dashboard is not necessarily innovative.

## Core business assessment

| Requirement | What an applicant should demonstrate |
| --- | --- |
| **Innovation** | A genuine original proposition with a defensible difference from existing alternatives |
| **Viability** | A credible founder/team, resources, delivery plan and realistic commercial assumptions |
| **Scalability** | A plausible strategy to grow, create jobs and expand to national or international markets |

These are the main venture tests described in the original handbook and in the current endorsement rules. An endorsement is not the same as a visa approval.


<a id="section-1"></a>

## 1. Decide whether this route fits

The source describes a two-stage decision: an authorised endorsing body assesses the venture, then UKVI makes the visa decision. The founder must actively develop and manage the UK business. Assess **innovation**, **viability** and **scalability** as separate tests. A company registration, website or untested AI feature does not prove them. The guide primarily covers a **new-business endorsement**; previously endorsed founders must review the separate same-business criteria.

<a id="section-2"></a>

## 2. Check your current immigration permission

Record the type of permission you currently hold, its expiry date, permitted work and self-employment conditions, and any implications for dependants. Determine whether you are eligible to switch inside the UK or must apply from abroad. **Do not begin activity that your current permission prohibits.** In particular, apply the current Student visa switching rules if applicable. Keep your immigration timeline separate from the time needed to develop the venture.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-3"></a>

## 3. Follow the application sequence

1. Check present immigration status, expiry, family circumstances and budget.
2. Choose a specific UK customer problem and research the market.
3. Prototype the core innovation and document your own role.
4. Prepare a business plan, three-year financial forecasts and evidence.
5. Select a currently authorised endorsing body and read its terms.
6. Apply for endorsement, complete due diligence and answer questions.
7. If successful, verify the endorsement letter and apply to UKVI before it expires.
8. Complete fees, healthcare surcharge, documents and identity checks.
9. After approval, maintain records and attend monitoring meetings.

This is a preparation sequence, not a guarantee or a fixed timetable.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-4"></a>

## 4. Choose a business problem worth testing

Select a sector you understand. Describe who experiences the problem, what they currently do, what fails, how often, and the impact. Interview buyers and actual users about recent experiences, current tools, purchasing authority and what would make them reject a solution. The source suggests **10–15 conversations** as a possible first-month planning target, **not** a visa threshold. Preserve dated notes with consent and protect personal information. Decide whether to revise the idea before paying to build a solution.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-5"></a>

## 5. Build evidence of innovation

Create a dated comparison with UK competitors, indirect alternatives and manual workarounds. Identify the distinct mechanism, the outcome it should improve and a way to measure it. Test a prototype and keep design history, technical results, team responsibilities and relevant IP or data-rights records. Show that the core innovation sits inside the business and is not simply an outsourced agency build, off-the-shelf AI wrapper or unsupported patent claim. Treat uncertain novelty and IP ownership honestly.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-6"></a>

## 6. Build evidence of viability

Connect the founding team's actual skills to delivery: technical implementation, customer knowledge, operations, research and commercial capability. Address gaps with genuine partners or credible hiring plans. Support forecasts with quotations, operating costs, funding sources and a delivery schedule. Use properly described interviews, procurement feedback, trials and pilot results as demand evidence; record negative feedback. Never treat a conditional investment promise as cash already available.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-7"></a>

## 7. Build evidence of scalability

Define the first UK customer segment, how customers will be acquired, and how the service will be delivered repeatedly without relying indefinitely on the founder's manual effort. Connect realistic growth stages to staffing, infrastructure, costs and expansion into other regions or markets. Present assumptions and constraints openly; projected headcount is not a guaranteed outcome.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-8"></a>

## 8. Use Nigeria experience to support the UK case

Document genuine business history, founder involvement, users, products, partnerships and evidence from Nigeria. Explain what transfers to the UK market and what still needs testing. Keep Nigerian traction separate from unproven UK demand. Check IP, data protection, cross-border operations and existing business ownership; do not disguise an established venture as a new UK business without telling the endorser.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-9"></a>

## 9. Three practical business development strategies

The source presents practical ways to grow a credible case before submission. Focus on discovering an actual customer need, building/test-driving the core proposition, and establishing relevant relationships and demand evidence. Set measurable learning objectives. Treat these as optional development approaches rather than official eligibility criteria.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-10"></a>

## 10. Find useful people and organisations

Prioritise people who can give genuine domain feedback: potential buyers, technical experts, professional organisations, university or innovation networks and local business support. Record what each person actually reviewed or contributed. A famous name or generic support email is not evidence of a customer commitment.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-11"></a>

## 11. Run and document a credible pilot

Agree a realistic use case with an appropriate participant, success measures, access permissions, trial boundaries and responsibilities. Log baseline, implementation, feedback, failed tests and actual results. Distinguish a prototype demonstration, free pilot, paid sale and fully deployed production service. Secure permission before sharing company or customer data.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-12"></a>

## 12. Prepare the business plan

Explain the UK customer problem, proposed innovation, market alternatives, founder contribution, team, route to market, delivery milestones, risks, compliance and financial assumptions. Align statements with actual prototypes, contracts and customer evidence. Use the particular endorsing body's current application template and page limits; a polished plan without supporting facts remains weak.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-13"></a>

## 13. Prepare realistic financial forecasts

Build a coherent three-year view of revenue drivers, pricing, volumes, customer acquisition, staff and technical costs, cash flow and funding. Record assumptions and present downside scenarios. Tie forecast growth to testing and sales evidence, not arbitrary percentages. The source's example amounts are planning illustrations, not Home Office financial benchmarks.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-14"></a>

## 14. Document funding and ownership

Record cash already available, investment conditions, owner contributions, shareholding, director and founder roles, intellectual property, licences and who controls the core technology. Keep supporting bank, corporate and contractual records private. Clearly differentiate a verified committed resource from a future intention.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-15"></a>

## 15. Choose an authorised endorsing body

Use the current government list of endorsing bodies; check which organisations accept **new applications** and whether their sector or criteria fit your business. Read their current document requests, fees, due diligence, interview and monitoring terms. A legacy endorsing body may be permitted only to support its existing endorsed founders. Do not pay an intermediary on the strength of an outdated list.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-16"></a>

## 16. Assemble the endorsement evidence pack

Follow the selected body's actual pack. Assemble a current business plan, evidence of innovation, feasibility and scalability, founder/team records, prototype and customer validation, funding and ownership information, forecasts, and requested identity or corporate documents. Label evidence clearly and eliminate contradictions between the form, plan and supporting material.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-17"></a>

## 17. Obtain useful customer and expert confirmations

Ask customers and genuine reviewers to describe specific interactions, tests, scope, dates and what they observed, rather than giving them fabricated wording or claims. Obtain appropriate consent for sharing identities or feedback. A letter of interest is different from a signed purchase order, and both are different from demonstrated revenue.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-18"></a>

## 18. Submit the endorsement application

Check the correct application route, currently authorised body, fees, evidence format, and all relevant declarations. Retain submitted versions and proof of payment or acknowledgement. Be ready to discuss the commercial model, originality, founder role, finances and risks. Do not assume endorsement is assured once a fee has been paid.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-19"></a>

## 19. Review the endorsement letter

If endorsed, confirm your name, passport and personal details, issuing body, endorsement reference, date and any case-specific information before UKVI submission. The current immigration rules require an endorsement letter issued within the relevant validity period (normally no more than **three months** before the visa application), and the endorsement must remain valid. Check the live rules and immediately raise any discrepancy with the issuer.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-20"></a>

## 20. Gather the visa documents

Follow the current UKVI checklist. The guide covers passport/identity, endorsement, English-language evidence, financial maintenance where required, tuberculosis certification where applicable, translations and other conditional materials. Keep the endorser's commercial documents separate from documents requested for the visa stage.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-21"></a>

## 21. Check maintenance and family documents

Determine whether a personal savings requirement applies and whether an exemption is available; check any partner and child maintenance and relationship requirements. Dependants must make appropriate applications and do not automatically obtain new permission when the founder switches. Do not publicise bank statements or identity documents in GitHub.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-22"></a>

## 22. Budget the full cost

Create a current budget for endorsement assessment and monitoring fees, potential VAT, visa application fee, Immigration Health Surcharge, document translations, TB checks if needed, travel and business-development costs. Use official current amounts and provider terms rather than relying on fees printed in a guide prepared in October 2026.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-23"></a>

## 23. Apply online from inside the UK

Confirm that the person can switch from their present permission and apply using the correct UKVI in-country form before their current permission expires. Upload documents and complete identity checks as instructed. Observe restrictions on travel while an in-country application is pending. Do not assume everyone's existing immigration category allows switching.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-24"></a>

## 24. Apply from Nigeria and complete identity checks

Use the correct application from outside the UK, provide the required evidence, and follow the stated identity or biometrics steps. Verify any tuberculosis testing requirement using approved providers and the live UKVI instructions. Plan for appointments and document lead times without assuming a fixed decision date.

The source sets out a **12-month optional development plan**, moving from idea validation and research into prototyping, piloting, commercial evidence and application preparation. Adapt it to your actual progress and immigration deadlines. Its month-by-month targets are planning suggestions, not mandatory requirements.

The source also provides a **24-month development plan** for founders needing more time to build technical proof, demand, operations, collaborators and credible scalability evidence. Do not apply simply because a calendar milestone has passed. Preserve honest measurements of what was delivered and what remains uncertain.

## 27. Record evidence with a simple tracker

Use the companion **Checklist** and **Progress log**. The Checklist has 20 items with fields for status, target date, saved file link and reminder; the Progress log records a dated real action/result, evidence link, next step and target date. Keep originals and identifiable evidence private. **Ready** is your own review status, not an endorsement or visa decision.

<a id="section-28"></a>

## 28. Final checklist before endorsement

Confirm your proposition is original, genuinely supported by evidence and capable of realistic execution and growth. Check founder involvement, ownership, operating costs, customer validation, business plan, forecasts, endorser authorisation, current conditions and complete document versions. Do not substitute polished marketing claims for verified proof.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

---

<a id="section-29"></a>

## 29. Final checklist before the visa application

Confirm valid endorsement letter, correct application type, passport, English evidence, maintenance or exemption, tuberculosis and certified translations where applicable, dependants documents where needed, and current fees. Check consistency of identities and dates. Submit within required validity windows.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-30"></a>

## 30. After approval and at monitoring meetings

Keep reliable records of trading activity, spending, hiring, customer evidence, operational development and progress against the endorsed business plan. Schedule the endorsing body's required monitoring/contact meetings and report genuine setbacks as well as progress. Follow any restrictions or further obligations attached to your permission.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-31"></a>

## 31. Understand settlement before relying on it

The route may lead to settlement, but the requirements are separate from the initial application. Check the current residence, endorsement, trading and objective achievement criteria before planning around any expected settlement date. Do not present a two- or three-year business plan as proof of a guaranteed right to remain.

[↑ Contents](#navigate-the-guide)

<a id="section-32"></a>

## 32. Resolve common problems

Where evidence is thin, revisit customer validation, technical delivery or genuine founder contribution rather than inventing proof. Where the business already operates abroad, explain it transparently. If a prospective body is not authorised, choose one from the official list. For visa status or ambiguous requirements, rely on official guidance and consider qualified regulated advice.
