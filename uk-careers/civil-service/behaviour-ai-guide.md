## 1. Understand what a behaviour actually assesses

A Civil Service behaviour assesses *actions and decisions* associated with performance. The [official Success Profiles behaviour definitions](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/success-profiles/success-profiles-civil-service-behaviours) vary by grade, and each vacancy selects the behaviours it needs. **You do not need to demonstrate every behaviour**, and a written example is not the only possible assessment.

| Common behaviour | Look for evidence of… | Weak evidence to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Communicating and Influencing | Audience understanding, message selection, feedback, disagreement handled and resulting decision | “I communicate well” without actions |
| Working Together | Genuine cooperation across different needs, shared objectives, resolving disagreement | A list of meetings without contribution |
| Making Effective Decisions | Options, evidence, consequences, risk, proportional judgement and result | A decision with no alternative or reasoning |
| Delivering at Pace | Priorities, dependencies, quality under constraint, escalation and delivery | Saying “I worked hard under pressure” |
| Changing and Improving | Identified problem, proposed intervention, testing, adoption and learning | Tool implementation without outcome or review |
| Leadership | Direction, inclusion, enablement and accountability appropriate to the grade | Claiming line management when you coordinated peers |

**Grade matters:** For example, HEO/SEO evidence may include coordinating delivery and engaging stakeholders; G7/G6 evidence generally needs more strategic responsibility, judgement and influence where the grade's published descriptors call for it. Do not claim a higher responsibility than you held.

## 2. Choose a real example before you write

Evaluate two or three real situations, then choose the most suitable for the *specific behaviour and grade*. A single complex story is not automatically better than a focused smaller one.

| Selection question | Example A | Example B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Directly matches named behaviour? | [Yes / partial / no] | […] |
| Shows **my** action and choices? | […] | […] |
| Suitable level of accountability? | […] | […] |
| Demonstrates risks/constraints and judgement? | […] | […] |
| Real, independently checkable result or learning? | […] | […] |
| Can be explained within actual word limit? | […] | […] |

If you have no example, look through legitimate volunteering, placements, study projects or work history. The Civil Service explicitly allows evidence from outside paid work. **Never manufacture an incident** to fill a behaviour.

## 3. Build the answer using STAR — then edit for relevance

| STAR stage | What the assessor needs to understand | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S — Situation** | Necessary context and significance | What problem, who was affected and why was it important? |
| **T — Task** | Your responsibility and objective | What were you accountable for and what constraint existed? |
| **A — Action** | **Your** reasoning, decisions and implementation | What did you do first, why, what alternatives/risks, who did you involve, how did you adapt? |
| **R — Result** | Actual outcome and evidence | What changed, how do you know, what learning or follow-up occurred? |

**A practical editing heuristic, not a scoring rule:** For a 250-word example you might allocate roughly 30–45 words for S/T, 150–175 for A and the remainder for R. Adjust to fit the actual question. Your Action needs enough detail for the reader to understand why your choices demonstrated the behaviour.

### Fictional illustration: Making Effective Decisions

**Unhelpful:** “I used data to make the right decision and received positive feedback.”

**Stronger, only if true:** “Two reporting datasets gave conflicting totals before a scheduled release. I traced the difference to an incomplete extract, compared the reconciliation results with the published specification and considered whether a partial report would mislead users. I recommended delaying the affected table while the team verified the replacement extract, documented the reason and informed the service lead of the impact. The validated table was released after the checks passed.”

This illustrates evidence, alternative options, consequences, communication and an outcome. It is fictional; it would need additional detail about the actual candidate's responsibility and confirmed results.

## 4. Question-first AI prompt

```text
Act as a Civil Service behaviour writing coach. I will supply:
- the exact job advert, grade and assessed behaviour;
- the official behaviour definition at that grade;
- the field's actual word count and name-blind/AI instructions;
- my real CV and a small set of factual work examples.

Do NOT draft immediately. Compare my examples against the behaviour definition and grade. Rank only by clear fit, verified personal actions, decision-making and result. Explain weaknesses and ask up to five questions about the selected example.

Check exact personal responsibility, relevant risks, options considered, collaboration, decisions, actions and what genuinely happened. Then create a STAR outline and a complete natural UK-English draft within the ACTUAL word limit (250 words only if the field specifies it).

Use appropriate grade-level evidence without inventing people-management, strategic authority, monetary impact, tools or numerical results. Apply anonymisation only as required by the advert. Keep Situation/Task concise; explain personal Action with reasons; give a defensible Result.

Finally return an evidence audit: which sentence maps to each behaviour requirement, unsupported claims, word count and remaining questions. Do not claim an official assessment score or guaranteed success.
```

## 5. Test your answer as an assessor would

| Test | Example of a problem | Concrete repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Behaviour relevance | “Delivered a dashboard” in a *Communicating* answer | Explain audience, differing views, how you adapted and influenced a decision |
| Personal ownership | “We created a process” | Clarify exactly what *you* proposed, tested, negotiated or approved |
| Reasoning | “I selected a solution” | State the meaningful alternatives, risks or trade-off |
| Grade fit | Senior answer describes only a minor administrative task | Choose a real higher-responsibility example or be honest about level |
| Impact | “Increased efficiency by 40%” with no source | Replace with an observed outcome or give actual baseline/measure |
| Unclear writing | Too many acronyms or stacked framework names | Write in simple spoken UK English |
| Excessive background | Half the answer is context | Shorten situation; protect the action detail |
| AI assumption | Names removed even when field requests them | Follow employer's specific anonymisation instructions |

### Five-minute self-test

Read the answer aloud and ask: **What did I decide? Why was it difficult? What did I do differently? What result is supported?** If a colleague unfamiliar with your work cannot answer, revise the action rather than adding grand adjectives. Ensure each behaviour answer remains distinct when the application asks for several.
