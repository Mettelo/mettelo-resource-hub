# NHS & Civil Service Interviews — Practical Preparation Guide

[🏠 Home](../../README.md) · [Start Here](../../START_HERE.md) · [Civil Service](README.md) · [NHS](../nhs/README.md) · [Career interviews](../../career/interviews/README.md)

**Mettelo · Revised 9 November 2026 · Prepared by Johnson Taiwo**

> **Using this guide:** Start with the actual job advert, essential criteria and applicant instructions. Examples are illustrative; never invent qualifications, responsibilities, outcomes or metrics. Employer requirements take priority.

## Begin with the invitation, not a question bank

Read the job description, person specification, interview invitation and any presentation/technical task instructions. The Civil Service may assess **behaviours, strengths, experience, technical skills or ability** in different combinations; NHS organisations may assess technical/professional competence and organisational values, with different assessment formats. [Civil Service Success Profiles](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/a-guide-to-civil-service-success-profiles/) · [NHS Employers values-based recruitment](https://www.nhsemployers.org/articles/values-based-recruitment).

### 1 · Build a one-page assessment plan

| Confirm from invitation | What to record | Specific preparation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role and grade/band | Exact position and employer | What work, risks and standards apply? |
| Assessment areas | Named behaviours, technical skills, person-specification requirements, Trust values | Find a genuine example or relevant explanation per area |
| Format | Panel, scenarios, presentation, demonstration, exercise, recorded questions | Practise the actual format |
| Timing | Duration, presentations, reading time and question limits if stated | Practise at realistic pace, not a fixed 2–3 minute rule |
| Accessibility / adjustments | Employer contact and stated adjustment route | Ask recruitment team in advance where required |
| Allowed materials and AI | What is permissible before and during interview | Do not use undisclosed live assistance in restricted assessments |

### 2 · Choose an evidence bank, not dozens of scripts

Select **five or six different real situations if available**, covering substantial work, delivery under constraint, collaboration, difficult judgement, service quality, improvement and learning. For each record your role, decisions and outcomes. A single example can be reused for practice, but avoid giving identical answers to several different questions.

| Example | Assessed area it might support | Your decision / personal action | Confirmed result | One likely follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Project A] | [Behaviour / service skill] | [Action and reason] | [Outcome] | Why not use another approach? |
| [Incident B] | [Risk, quality, communication] | [Trade-off and escalation] | [Outcome] | What would you change? |

**Never invent technical, clinical or financial achievements** simply because an interview pack asks for a “strong example”.

## Civil Service preparation

### Behaviour questions

Match the **exact named behaviour and grade** to the [official behaviour framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/success-profiles/success-profiles-civil-service-behaviours). Practise a concise Situation/Task, detailed personal Actions and Result, then answer follow-ups about options, risk, judgement and stakeholder response. Grade 7 answers may need more substantial strategic decisions and breadth of consequences where genuinely present than EO examples; don't inflate responsibility.

### Strength questions

The employer may assess what comes naturally, what motivates you and what you do well regularly. Reflect honestly on real work preferences; **do not memorise a rigid STAR story for every strength question**. Official Civil Service guidance notes that strengths look for a genuine initial response.

### Technical questions or case studies

Prepare a **plain-English explanation**: problem → assumptions → options → choice → implementation → testing/assurance → risks → result/limitations. For an engineering question, explain failure handling, idempotency or monitoring only if relevant; for policy/service roles, explain governance or service impact. Do not present imaginary platforms as your own experience.

### Practice prompt — Civil Service

```text
Prepare me for this Civil Service interview using the actual vacancy, grade, invitation, named Success Profile assessment elements and my genuine experience.

First build a table with the EXACT ASSESSED AREA, the likely type of question (not a predicted panel question), and the best real supporting example. Ask targeted questions where ownership, decision, technical method or result is unclear.

For each area, propose one or two realistic practice questions. For behaviour questions, develop a spoken STAR answer with a concise situation, my specific actions and decisions, risks/trade-offs and the genuine result. For strengths, coach authentic concise reflection rather than a memorised script. For technical questions, explain the concept in plain English with options and limitations, then use my actual experience where relevant.

Include the exact question and assessed area inside each reusable practice answer. After each answer, provide two challenging panel follow-up questions and an evidence/clarity check. Respect the interview format, confidentiality and AI restrictions. Do not invent employer questions or score me using an official rubric.
```

## NHS preparation

### Understand the actual service and person specification

Find the organisation's published values, service context and the **specific Band/role criteria**, and map practical examples. A digital NHS analyst does not need to invent patient-facing duties; a healthcare support worker should describe observation, documentation and escalation within their actual scope; a registered practitioner should discuss their real competencies and registration truthfully.

### Values-based and situational questions

Prepare examples showing **how you behaved**, not a speech that repeats Trust values. An NHS values question might explore how you communicated respectfully, responded to a service-user need, worked with colleagues or corrected an error. In a scenario, explain what information you would check, who you would involve, whether you would escalate, and how you would follow the organisation's protocols—**never improvise clinical instructions beyond your role**.

### Practice prompt — NHS

```text
Help me prepare for an NHS interview for [EXACT ROLE, BAND, TRUST]. Use the full advert, person specification, Trust values, invitation and my verified work history.

First identify which requirements the panel may assess, separating professional/technical, safety/governance, values, service improvement and teamwork according to the actual job. Do not assume clinical duties for non-clinical positions.

Generate up to two relevant practice questions per major criterion. For each question, show the EXACT QUESTION and assessed criterion, select one real example, ask me questions about my own actions and results, then develop a clear spoken STAR or scenario answer as appropriate. Include two follow-ups on decision making, escalation, communication, data confidentiality or learning where applicable.

Do not invent registration, safeguarding action, patient outcomes, qualifications or metrics. Check how the answer reflects the actual organisation's published values using actions rather than slogans. Respect the interview's time/AI rules; help me rehearse without memorising.
```

## 3 · What a clear interview answer sounds like

**Fictional illustration, relevant to a non-clinical quality role:**

**Question:** “Tell us about a time you identified a quality problem and took action.”

**Assessed area:** Quality assurance and communication.

**Response pattern:** “During preparation of a scheduled report, I spotted a mismatch between two totals. My responsibility was to verify the data before publication. I checked the source records and traced the mismatch to an incomplete extract, documented the exception, and discussed options with the reporting lead. We agreed to hold the affected table while the correct extract was reprocessed. I reran the reconciliation and recorded the checks once the totals matched. The validated report was then released.”

**Potential follow-up:** “What would you do if the deadline could not move?” Explain real risk/decision authority, not an invented governance approval.

This pattern describes decisions and assurance. Replace it with your own case, actual role and observed result.

## 4 · Mock interview — a practical 30-minute routine

1. **Five minutes:** explain the role and why your evidence fits, in ordinary language.
2. **Ten minutes:** answer two relevant behaviour/experience questions with follow-ups.
3. **Ten minutes:** explain a technical, scenario or service judgement question relevant to your assessed areas.
4. **Five minutes:** review one thing unclear, one unsupported claim and one missed decision; repeat the answer more simply.

**Assess yourself on clarity, truth, relevance, explanation of actions, reasoning and real outcome.** This is a practice checklist—not a purported government score.

## 5 · Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Better action |
| --- | --- |
| Memorising 20 long scripts | Know your 5–6 real cases and practise adapting |
| Describing “we” without your specific contribution | Explain what *you* decided, delivered, checked or escalated |
| Giving a complete technical lecture | Answer the exact question at the panel's likely level; offer details when asked |
| NHS values presented as slogans | Describe a real behavioural decision relevant to service users |
| Invented “patient care impact” from an administrative job | Discuss actual administrative reliability, confidentiality and service contribution |
| Treating strengths as lengthy STAR questions | Reflect naturally on actual motivation and ways of working |
| Assuming names must be hidden in a spoken interview | Follow the interview instructions |
| Inventing expected interview questions | Treat proposed questions as realistic preparation only |
| Uploading confidential work documents to AI | Use synthetic or permission-cleared examples |
