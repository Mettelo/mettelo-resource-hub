1.2 THE CIVIL SERVICE SCORING
FRAMEWORK
Civil Service roles are assessed using the Success Profiles model. Applicants are scored on a 1–7 scale.

CIVIL SERVICE 1–7 SCORING SCALE

                    Score               Meaning                    Panel Interpretation

                   1           Not Demonstrated      No relevant evidence

                   2           Minimal               Weak or incomplete example

                   3           Acceptable            Basic evidence, limited depth

                   4           Good                  Clear structure, relevant example

                   5           Strong                Clear ownership and measurable outcome

                   6           Very Strong           Complex example, strong judgement

                   7           Outstanding           Exceptional impact, strategic level


WHAT PANELS LOOK FOR

Civil Service panels assess:

   •   Behaviour demonstration
   •   Level of autonomy
   •   Decision-making quality
   •   Strategic awareness
   •   Scale of impact

A score of 4 often reflects a well-structured example. A score of 5+ demonstrates ownership, measurable results
and complexity appropriate to grade.


1.3 THE NHS SHORTLISTING FRAMEWORK
NHS recruitment follows the Agenda for Change structure (for most non-medical roles). Unlike the Civil
Service numerical behaviour scoring system, NHS shortlisting focuses on:

       •   Essential criteria
       •   Desirable criteria
       •   Band-level responsibility
       •   Service impact

NHS SHORTLISTING LOGIC

Step 1: Does the candidate meet all essential criteria?
Step 2: Is the level of responsibility aligned to the band?
Step 3: Do examples demonstrate measurable service impact?

If essential criteria are not clearly evidenced, applications are often rejected regardless of other strengths.


1.4 BAND-LEVEL VS GRADE-LEVEL
EXPECTATIONS
Both systems assess “level”, but they describe it differently.

CIVIL SERVICE (GRADE-BASED)

   •   EO / HEO: Operational delivery with defined responsibility
   •   SEO / G7: Strategic contribution and independent judgement
   •   G6+: Senior leadership and policy influence

NHS (BAND-BASED)

   •   Band 5–6: Technical competence and supervised responsibility
   •   Band 7: Team leadership and service development
   •   Band 8a+: Strategic oversight and cross-functional impact

Panels do not only assess what you did. They assess what level you operated.

1.6 WHAT MOVES AN APPLICATION FROM
AVERAGE TO COMPETITIVE
Most applicants score at the equivalent of:

   •   Civil Service: 3–4
   •   NHS: “Meets minimum criteria”

Why?

Because they:

   •   Describe activity instead of impact
   •   Fail to quantify results
   •   Do not clarify personal contribution
   •   Ignore grade or band expectations

A competitive application:

   •   Demonstrates ownership
   •   Shows judgement
   •   Quantifies improvement
   •   Aligns evidence to required level

This blueprint will teach you how to structure your responses to meet both systems confidently.

In this system, AI is positioned as a:

✔ Structuring assistant

strategically.

• Convert unstructured notes into structured responses
 • Improve sentence clarity
 • Reduce repetition
 • Suggest stronger action verbs
 • Highlight missing measurable outcomes
 • Reformat answers into STAR structure
 • Check alignment against criteria

2.4 WHAT AI MUST NOT DO
AI must never:

• Invent examples
• Exaggerate responsibility level
• Generate false metrics
• Recreate confidential NHS or government data
• Rewrite into overly generic language
• Remove your authentic voice

Submitting fabricated information in a public sector application can result in:

   •   Application rejection
   •   Withdrawal of offer
   •   Potential disciplinary or reputational consequences

Professional integrity is non-negotiable.

2.6 AI TONE AND AUTHENTICITY
WARNING
Panels are increasingly familiar with AI-generated writing patterns.

Common red flags include:

• Overly polished language inconsistent with CV
 • Americanised terminology
 • Generic leadership phrases
 • Abstract statements without specifics

If your response sounds detached from your real voice, revise it. Clarity is valued more than sophistication.


2.7 DATA PROTECTION AND
CONFIDENTIALITY
When drafting applications:

Do not include:

   •   Identifiable patient data
   •   Sensitive government data
   •   Confidential internal reports
   •   Security-sensitive information

Replace specifics with structured descriptions, such as:

“Improved reporting accuracy across a regional service”
 instead of
 “Improved reporting accuracy for X Trust's cancer unit dataset containing 12,000 records.”

Maintain professionalism and compliance at all times.

3.4 THE S.T.A.R.I.M BULLET FORMULA
Each employment bullet should follow this structure:

Action + Context + Method + Result + Impact + Metric

Example (Civil Service style - Blind):

“Led redesign of a departmental performance reporting framework within a Central Government Department,

compliance, and improved executive-level decision-making turnaround from weekly to daily.”

“Implemented a data quality improvement initiative within an NHS Trust’s operational reporting system,
introducing structured validation protocols that reduced discrepancies by 38%, improved patient pathway
tracking accuracy, and enhanced divisional performance oversight.”

   •      Clear ownership
   •      Explicit action
   •      Governance awareness
   •      Measurable outcome
   •      Organisational impact

4.1 THE PURPOSE OF THE PERSONAL STATEMENT

In both:

   •   UK Civil Service
   •   National Health Service

the personal or supporting statement is a primary shortlisting tool.

It is not a narrative biography.

It is a structured evidence document used to assess:

   •   Alignment to essential criteria
   •   Behaviour demonstration (Civil Service)
   •   Band-level or grade-level readiness
   •   Measurable impact
   •   Organisational fit

A strong statement is deliberate, structured and evidence-led.

4.2: THE A.C.E.I.A™ PERSONAL
STATEMENT FRAMEWORK
To ensure consistency and scoring strength, apply the:


A.C.E.I.A™ FRAMEWORK
A – Alignment
C – Core Evidence
E – Explicit Criteria Mapping
I – Impact & Metrics
A – Authority & Level Confirmation

This framework ensures clarity, structure, and measurable value.


A – ALIGNMENT

Open with a concise introduction summarising:

   •   Motivation to serve

NHS emphasis:

   •   Service ethos
   •   Patient-centred commitment

C – CORE EVIDENCE

Structure the body into thematic paragraphs aligned directly to the job description.

Each paragraph should:

   •   Begin with a strong topic sentence
   •   Contain structured examples (using S.T.A.R.I.M logic)
   •   Demonstrate ownership and measurable outcomes

Panels must be able to clearly see that you meet every essential criterion. Do not assume alignment will be
inferred. Where appropriate, signal alignment explicitly, for example:

“This experience directly demonstrates my ability to meet the essential requirement for…”

Ensure all essential criteria are addressed clearly and proportionately.

5.1 WHY TRADITIONAL STAR IS NO LONGER ENOUGH

The traditional STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is widely used in public sector recruitment.

However, applications to:

   •   UK Civil Service
   •   National Health Service

are assessed not only on structured examples, but on measurable organisational value and responsibility level.
Standard STAR responses often describe activity.

High-scoring responses demonstrate impact.

5.2 THE S.T.A.R.I.M™ FRAMEWORK
EXPLAINED
S – Situation: Provide brief and relevant context. Avoid excessive background detail.

T – Task: Clarify your specific responsibility. What were you accountable for? What was expected of you?

A – Action: This is the most important section. Clearly explain what you personally did. Outline decisions
made, prioritisation, stakeholder engagement, governance considerations, tools or methodologies applied, and
how you ensured quality and compliance.

R – Result: Describe the immediate outcome of your actions.

I – Impact: Explain the wider organisational, operational, or service-level effect. What changed beyond the
immediate task?

M – Metrics: Provide measurable evidence where credible. Include percentages, time reductions, cost savings,
risk mitigation, performance improvements, or quality indicators.

Together, these six elements create a complete evidence narrative.
