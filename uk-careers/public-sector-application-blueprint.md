## Part 1. Understand how the application is assessed

### Civil Service Success Profiles

The official [Civil Service Success Profiles](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/a-guide-to-civil-service-success-profiles/) comprise **behaviours, strengths, ability, technical skills and experience**. Individual vacancies choose relevant elements and assessment methods. The official [behaviours](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/success-profiles/success-profiles-civil-service-behaviours) are grade-specific. A personal statement is commonly used to show evidence against essential criteria, but format and word limits vary. Civil Service scoring systems must be read from the *actual* assessment and feedback provided; a generic 1–7 “panel interpretation” table is a **Mettelo simplification, not a universal official scoring rubric**.

**What to do:** Highlight essential criteria and specifically assessed behaviours/technical areas, then map each to a true example. Do not attempt to address every Success Profile element unless the advert asks for it.

### NHS shortlisting and band-level evidence

NHS organisations usually use the job description and **person specification** to decide what experience, knowledge, qualifications and behaviours matter. The [NHS Terms and Conditions Handbook](https://www.nhsemployers.org/publications/tchandbook) discusses genuine minimum requirements and the importance of person specifications. Employers may use values-based approaches; the published Trust values and specific role responsibilities matter. **NHS scoring methods and form fields vary**, so don't assume an automatic numerical scale or identical supporting-information requirements.

**What to do:** List every essential criterion in the person specification and identify where your actual work demonstrates it. Check mandatory registration, training or statutory status separately.

### A reusable requirement coverage table

| Requirement exactly as written | Where it is assessed | My real supporting evidence | Evidence strength | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Essential skill or qualification] | Employment history / statement / interview | [Role or credential] | Direct / transferable / missing | [Action] |
| [Relevant technical requirement] | [Field] | [Actual project and method] | [Status] | [Action] |
| [Communication or values] | [Field] | [Actual relevant example] | [Status] | [Action] |

**A useful distinction:** *Evidence coverage* does not mean repeating keywords. An assessor should be able to see **what you personally did, how you did it, and what genuinely resulted**.

## Part 2. Safe AI and candidate responsibility

Use the AI-safe framework as a **verification process**. AI should help organise facts, ask better questions and improve clarity; it should never invent details.

### Safe six-stage method

1. **Prepare** the complete advert and relevant instructions, and remove personal/customer/patient/employer secrets before using an AI tool.
2. **Extract** the employer's actual requirements and flag anything that is an interpretation, not stated.
3. **Build** a role-by-role factual evidence bank with tools, methods, decisions, risks, stakeholders and outcomes.
4. **Clarify** uncertain points through short questions instead of using AI to fill gaps.
5. **Draft** using only the approved evidence and within stated limits.
6. **Audit** the draft for ownership, dates, credentials, confidentiality, unsupported metrics, natural language and assessment coverage.

### False precision to avoid

| Unsafe instruction | Better instruction |
| --- | --- |
| “Increase measurable results” | “Check whether a real baseline, comparison and period exist; otherwise use a factual qualitative outcome.” |
| “Make the role sound more senior” | “Explain only the independence, decisions and accountability actually held.” |
| “Use six STAR examples per paragraph” | “Choose one or two directly relevant examples as the word limit allows.” |
| “Always remove employers for Civil Service” | “Follow this vacancy/field's name-blind instructions exactly.” |
| “Always retain employer names for NHS” | “Follow this employer's application instructions exactly.” |
| “Make each bullet at least 25 words” | “Use enough words to show useful evidence, without padding.” |

### AI fact-check prompt

```text
Act as a factual auditor of my public-sector application. I have supplied the actual vacancy, person specification, submitted CV and my own verified evidence notes.

Before drafting, extract the essential criteria and field-level instructions. Mark information as explicitly stated by the employer, inferred for discussion, or not yet known.

Review each proposed claim. Ask what my personal responsibility was, which actions or decisions I actually took, what method I used, which risk or stakeholder was involved, and what the confirmed result was. If a metric has no source/period, flag it as UNSUPPORTED.

Do not invent clinical actions, safeguarding responsibility, seniority, credentials, results, employer names or percentages. Explain where separate NHS and Civil Service rules apply. Draft only once I confirm the evidence. At the end, produce an exact criterion-to-sentence coverage map and outstanding questions. Do not claim to know the assessor's score.
```

**Source safety:** [NHS Employers AI guidance](https://www.nhsemployers.org/articles/guidance-use-artificial-intelligence-candidate-applications). Always comply with the employer's assessment and AI rules.

## Part 3. Employment history with genuine evidence

Good employment history distinguishes genuine work from vague personal claims. But not every employment-history field is an essay: some ask only for dates and core duties.

### Separate your work by role

| Field | Record for each real job |
| --- | --- |
| Role, employer, dates | Exact contractual title and timeline |
| Regular responsibilities | Work you were authorised and expected to perform |
| Significant incidents/projects | Distinct situations relevant to target role |
| Personal action and decision | What *you* did versus what the team did |
| Professional standards | Testing, policy, quality assurance, ethics, escalation or safety actually relevant |
| Outcome | Output delivered, measurable result **if true**, or a verified non-numerical outcome |

**Illustrative, fictional rewrite:**

*Vague:* “Produced reports, supported the team and improved performance.”

*More informative:* “Reconciled weekly data extracts against agreed totals, logged inconsistent records and worked with the service lead to correct the published definitions before the reporting cycle.”

The rewrite explains process, ownership and quality. It is **not** a candidate's actual accomplishment.

### Civil Service versus NHS history

For **Civil Service**, check whether the field is a name-blind CV, a role-history form or a separate experience example. For **NHS**, use the employer's form and describe genuine care, administration, digital, operational or professional practice. **Neither sector has a universal rule requiring a set number or length of bullets.**

Detailed companion: [Employment History Guide](civil-service/employment-history-guide.md).

## Part 4. Personal and supporting statements

Build every statement around genuine evidence against the actual essential criteria. That remains the foundation; rigid 750/1,000/1,450-word targets do not.

### A clear paragraph plan

| Section | Aim | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | Identify genuine role-relevant experience and motivation briefly | Why this job, and what relevant work can I substantiate? |
| First evidence section | Address the most important essential requirements | What was the problem, my action, the reasoning and observed outcome? |
| Next sections | Cover other distinct requirement groups | Can I show another real aspect of responsibility without repetition? |
| Values/working style (if assessed) | Demonstrate behaviour through actual work | How did I act, collaborate or manage risk? |
| Closing | Confirm role fit concisely | What specific strengths remain most relevant? |

### NHS example selection

Use person-specification order where helpful. A candidate for an NHS analyst role might show validation, interpretation, confidential-data handling and communication to clinicians or service managers *only when true*. A healthcare support applicant might demonstrate safe documentation, communication and escalation **within their actual scope**. [NHS Trust application example](https://www.covwarkpt.nhs.uk/application-guidance-).

### Civil Service example selection

Match the named essential criteria and grade, including technical or experience requirements. One solid example can cover multiple criteria if the links are explicit. Do not use an instruction to draw all examples from the current role; older, voluntary or project work may be stronger and legitimate.

Detailed companion: [Supporting and Personal Statement Guide](civil-service/personal-statement-guide.md).

## Part 5. STAR and the Mettelo S.T.A.R.I.M™ method

**STAR** means **Situation, Task, Action, Result**. The Civil Service recommends using STAR when preparing behavioural examples. Mettelo's original blueprint extends STAR into **S.T.A.R.I.M™**, encouraging applicants to distinguish additional **Impact** and **Measurement** in their evidence notes.

**Important:** S.T.A.R.I.M™ is a **Mettelo editorial method, not an official Civil Service or NHS assessment framework**. It does not replace the vacancy's criteria or require six headings in the final response.

| Component | Plain meaning | Useful question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Situation** | What was happening | What was the service need or problem? |
| **Task** | Your responsibility | What outcome were you accountable for? |
| **Action** | Your personal method and judgement | What exactly did you do and why? |
| **Result** | What actually happened | What deliverable, resolution or change occurred? |
| **Impact** | Why that result mattered | Who benefited and what organisational or service issue was addressed? |
| **Measurement** | What reliable evidence supports the effect | What real before/after, count, test, evaluation or qualitative confirmation exists? |

**Avoid double-counting:** Result, Impact and Measurement may overlap. In a short written answer, show the information naturally rather than repeating it under three headings.

### A practical transformation (fictional)

*Weak:* “Led improvements in monthly reporting, resulting in better quality.”

*Evidence developed in private notes:* Several teams used conflicting report definitions. Applicant clarified requirements, created validation checks and obtained service-owner approval. Actual outcome: definitions agreed, checks recorded, reporting issued with documented method. **No verified error-rate reduction exists.**

*Clear application line:* “I reconciled conflicting monthly reporting definitions with service leads, introduced documented validation checks and confirmed the revised measures before publication, enabling teams to use consistent figures.”

This is stronger because the method and result are explained. A fictional example might mention a **42% reduction**; that number was illustrative and must never be copied into a real application without source evidence.

### Use STAR and S.T.A.R.I.M™ intelligently

| Document | Recommended use |
| --- | --- |
| Employment history | Condense the logic into a natural bullet; do not write six labelled paragraphs per role |
| Personal/supporting statement | Use evidence paragraphs grouped by criteria, not formulaic headings everywhere |
| Civil Service behaviour | STAR remains a useful concise structure; explain personal Action and substantiated Result |
| Interview | Adapt real examples in spoken language and prepare for follow-up questions |

## Part 6. What commonly goes wrong and how to fix it

| Failure | Why an assessor may struggle | Specific repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic claims | “I demonstrate leadership” does not show what you did | Describe a real decision, whom it affected and what followed |
| Missing essential criterion | Shortlister cannot locate proof of requirement | Map criterion to an exact sentence or a truthful missing gap |
| Unsupported numerical impact | Claim looks unverifiable or misleading | Confirm source, denominator, baseline and period, otherwise remove |
| Inflated job grade / band | Described authority does not match real job | Select example showing your genuine scope; avoid title inflation |
| NHS values as slogans | No behavioural evidence | Show action demonstrating the actual published Trust value |
| Clinical responsibilities invented | Candidate could misrepresent safety/professional competence | Describe exact permitted work, escalation and authority |
| CS name-blind rules applied everywhere | Could erase required employment information | Follow each field's actual instructions |
| Overly complex AI prose | Reader cannot understand contribution | Short sentences, fewer buzzwords and actual process details |
| Entire CV repeated as statement | Little proof against criteria | Use specific evidence linked to person specification |
| Same STAR story reused without adaptation | Distinct criteria not demonstrated | Select a different example or explain the different relevant dimension |

### Final application audit

- [ ] The correct advert and version of the person specification were used.
- [ ] Every actual essential criterion is addressed where met; missing criteria remain honestly identified.
- [ ] Required qualifications, registration, safeguarding, clearance and eligibility are accurate.
- [ ] Employment dates and roles match the history and CV.
- [ ] Decisions, tools, governance and impact statements reflect the applicant's actual work.
- [ ] Every numerical outcome has a trustworthy basis.
- [ ] The writing is clear, appropriately detailed and not repetitive.
- [ ] Anonymous fields, length, file formats and AI restrictions follow the vacancy.
- [ ] Confidential, personal, patient and employer-protected data are excluded.
- [ ] Applicant has personally reviewed the final submission.
